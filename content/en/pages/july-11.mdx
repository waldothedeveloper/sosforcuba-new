On July 11, 2021, demonstrations spread from San Antonio de los Baños to communities across Cuba. Participants called for freedom, expressed anger over living conditions, and challenged a political system built to prevent nationwide public dissent.

The events are easiest to understand as four connected chapters.

## 1. A country took to the streets

Videos from participants showed demonstrations across more than 90 reported locations. The geographic breadth distinguished July 11 from smaller, locally contained acts of dissent.

[Read the protest timeline →](/articles/july-11-2021)

## 2. The government mobilized a response

President Miguel Díaz-Canel called supporters into the streets and state forces confronted demonstrators. Recordings documented beatings, arrests, and the deployment of police and specialized units.

[Read about the government response →](/articles/cuban-government-response)

## 3. Communications were restricted

Internet and platform restrictions made it harder for Cubans to organize, publish evidence, and locate family members. The state’s control over ETECSA gave it a powerful instrument for limiting visibility.

[Read about the internet shutdown →](/articles/internet-blackout-july-2021)

## 4. Arrests continued after the streets cleared

Families searched for missing relatives while activists tracked detentions and legal proceedings. Artists, journalists, students, political opponents, and ordinary participants were among those arrested.

[Read the preserved detainee stories →](/articles/missing-and-detained-after-july-11)

July 11 is history, but it is not finished history. Prison sentences, exile, censorship, and family separation continued long after the first videos disappeared from international headlines.

On July 11, President Miguel Díaz-Canel appeared on national television as demonstrations spread across Cuba. Rather than acknowledge peaceful demands, he called government supporters into the streets.

> “The order of combat has been given. Revolutionaries, to the streets.”

## Who was deployed

The people described as “revolutionaries” included organized state forces and government supporters mobilized to confront demonstrators.

### Special military brigades

Units known as the **Black Berets**—*boinas negras*—were deployed during the response.

Videos and photographs also showed government-aligned civilians carrying sticks in the streets. Independent reporting and recordings documented beatings and arrests as the response intensified.

## Documented violence

The U.S. Department of the Treasury later described the deployment of the National Revolutionary Police to suppress protesters. Its statement cited confrontations, arrests, the beating and arrest of a Catholic priest defending young protesters in Camagüey, and recorded instances of police using clubs against peaceful demonstrators.

<SourceLink href="https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy0298">U.S. Treasury statement on the July 2021 response</SourceLink>

The climate extended beyond people who had demonstrated. Cubans reported being afraid to take out their phones in public because police might assume they were recording evidence against the government.

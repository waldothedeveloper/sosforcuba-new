In the days after July 11, members of the National Revolutionary Police, riot units, and State Security carried out arrests across Cuba. Families and civil-society groups searched for people whose locations were unknown.

This page preserves several individual stories documented on the original SOS for Cuba site. Statuses can change; current information should always be checked with active prisoner-monitoring organizations.

## Anyelo Troya González

Cuban photographer **Anyelo Troya González** documented the demonstrations on July 11. He was later detained and sentenced to one year in prison after a summary proceeding, without a defense lawyer present, for filming the protests.

<SourceLink href="https://twitter.com/AnyeloTroyaGonz">Anyelo Troya González’s public profile</SourceLink>

## Dina Stars

Cuban YouTuber **Dina Stars** was detained by state security while appearing on live television and discussing the arrests of activists, demonstrators, and journalists.

> “I hold the government responsible for anything that may happen to me.”

Moments later, she told the program’s host: “I have to go. They told me to accompany them.”

<SourceLink href="https://www.npr.org/2021/07/14/1015837541/a-popular-youtuber-is-detained-by-cuban-police-on-live-tv-in-a-roundup-of-critic">NPR report on the live detention</SourceLink>

## Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara

Artist and San Isidro Movement leader **Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara** published a video on July 11 saying he was going into the streets and calling for democracy. He was detained and transferred to a maximum-security prison.

## José Daniel Ferrer García

**José Daniel Ferrer García**, a human-rights activist and leader of the Patriotic Union of Cuba (UNPACU), was detained on July 11. His family reported that authorities denied them contact and access to information about his condition.

<MediaImage src="/media/legacy/jdferrer.jpg" alt="Cuban human-rights activist José Daniel Ferrer García" caption="José Daniel Ferrer García, leader of UNPACU." />

His wife and son publicly demanded access to him and said he was being held in solitary confinement.

## Journalists and bystanders

The repression also affected people documenting events. Spanish-born Associated Press photographer **Ramon Espinosa** was attacked by police while covering a demonstration in Havana. Independent journalists, activists, artists, students, and minors were among those reported detained.
